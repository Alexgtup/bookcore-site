The Black Swan

Nassim Nicholas Taleb · 2007

A critique of overconfident prediction in domains where rare, high-impact events dominate outcomes.

Key ideas ↓Русская версия
In one sentence

Be humble about forecasts and build exposure so surprises hurt less and useful upside remains open.

Why it mattersCore ideasTry it

Why it matters

Historical averages can create false comfort when a small number of extreme events determine most of the result.

Core ideas

What is worth remembering after you close the page

01

Rare events dominate

In some domains, the average hides the events that actually shape the outcome.

02

Stories arrive after events

Humans are good at explaining surprises once they have already happened.

03

Exposure matters more than prediction

You can often improve outcomes by changing what happens when you are wrong.

03
Turn it into action

Three things to test today

  1. 1

    Identify where one extreme event could dominate your plan.

  2. 2

    Stress-test the plan without assigning a precise probability.

  3. 3

    Cap downside while leaving some room for unexpected upside.
